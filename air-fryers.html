How to Choose an Air Fryer

An air fryer is a small countertop oven that circulates hot air to crisp food with little oil. Models differ more than the marketing suggests, and the wrong size is the most common regret. These are the points that decide which one fits your kitchen.

The short answer

  • Best for most kitchens: A 4 to 6 quart basket model
  • Best for two foods at once: A dual-basket air fryer
  • Best for small spaces: A compact 2 to 3 quart model
  • Best for baking and big batches: An oven-style air fryer

What to look for

Capacity

  • 2 to 3 quarts: one person, or a couple with small portions.
  • 4 to 6 quarts: the right size for most households of two to four.
  • 7 quarts and up, or dual basket: families, or cooking two foods at once.

Basket or oven style

Basket models are compact and heat quickly. Oven-style models with racks hold more and suit baking and reheating, but they need more counter space. Dual-basket models cook two items at different settings and can finish them together.

Power and temperature range

Most home units run around 1,400 to 1,800 watts. A top temperature of about 400°F (200°C) gives better crisping. Check that the plug and voltage in the listing match your country before you buy.

Controls

Dials are simple and durable. Digital screens add presets, which only help if you will use them. Look for a shake reminder and automatic shut-off.

Cleaning

A dishwasher-safe nonstick basket saves effort. Read reviews for comments on coating wear, the most common long-term complaint.

Common questions

Is an air fryer healthier?

It uses far less oil than deep frying, so fried-style foods can have fewer calories, but the food you cook still matters most.

Can I use foil or parchment?

Usually yes, if it does not block airflow and is weighed down by food. Check your manual.

How do I choose between brands?
