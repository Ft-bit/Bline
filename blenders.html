How to Choose a Blender

Blenders range from small single-serve cups to powerful machines that make nut butter. The right one depends on what you blend most often, so start there instead of with the price tag.

The short answer

  • Best for daily smoothies: A personal blender
  • Best all-rounder: A full-size countertop blender, 1,000 watts or more
  • Best for soups and small jobs: An immersion (stick) blender
  • Best for nut butter and ice: A high-performance blender

What to look for

Motor power

Around 600 watts handles soft fruit and shakes. 1,000 watts and up copes better with ice, frozen fruit and fibrous greens.

Jar material

Glass resists scratches and stains but is heavy. BPA-free plastic is light and shatter-resistant but can cloud over time.

Blade and jar shape

A wide, tapered jar pulls food toward the blades. Check that the blade assembly can be removed for cleaning.

Noise and cleaning

All blenders are loud, but heavier bases and lids tend to be quieter. Look for dishwasher-safe jars and a self-clean cycle with warm water and a drop of dish soap.

Common questions

Can I blend hot liquids?

Only if the manual says so. Fill the jar no more than halfway and vent the lid, since steam builds pressure.

Blender or food processor?

A blender is for liquids and smoothies. A food processor is for chopping, slicing and doughs.
